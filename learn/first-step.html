Your first step — how to actually start

The practical part. What to open, what you need, and how to make your first investment this month.
Most people stall here. They understand inflation, they accept they should invest, and then nothing happens for two years because nobody told them exactly what to do on Monday morning.

Start with the simplest instrument

For a first investment, the FGN Savings Bond is hard to beat. The minimum is ₦5,000. It is backed by the federal government. The rate is fixed and known before you commit. Interest arrives in your account every three months.

It will not make you rich. That is not its job. Its job is to get you invested, to pay you meaningfully more than a savings account, and to show you that this is not complicated.

What ₦100,000 looks like

₦100,000 in a 3-year FGN Savings Bond at 14.071%, October’s rate: Every quarter → ₦3,518 into your account Every year → ₦14,071 Over three years → ₦42,213 in interest At maturity → your ₦100,000 returned in full

What you need and where to go

You need a bank account in your own name, your BVN, and a valid ID. That is it.

Subscriptions go through a licensed stockbroker or your bank’s investment desk. Most commercial banks have one — ask for the treasury or investment department rather than the regular counter, since branch staff often do not handle this.

The window opens roughly once a month for about a week. Miss it and you wait for the next one. Yield will tell you when it opens.

The sequence

1. Wait for the Yield alert that the window is open 2. Call your bank’s investment desk or a stockbroker 3. Ask to subscribe to the current FGN Savings Bond 4. Confirm the tenor (2 or 3 years) and the amount 5. Fund it before the window closes

Check yourself

You want to start investing but only have ₦20,000 spare. What can you do?

  • Nothing — you need at least ₦500,000 to begin
  • Subscribe to an FGN Savings Bond, which starts at ₦5,000
  • Wait until you have saved more
Show the answer

Right. The ₦5,000 minimum exists precisely so ordinary people can participate. ₦20,000 is a real starting position — and starting small now beats starting large later, because the years you give it are worth more than the amount.

Do this now

Turn on the alert for the next FGN Savings Bond window — it is linked just below, with its dates and rate.

Then call your bank and ask one question: "Does your investment desk handle FGN Savings Bond subscriptions?" That single call removes the largest obstacle between you and your first investment.
