Saving vs investing — the real difference

Both are sensible. They solve different problems. Using the wrong one costs you.
Chidi keeps everything in one savings account — his emergency fund, his rent money, and the ₦2,000,000 he will not need for five years. All of it earning 7.46%. Two of those three decisions are wrong.

Two different jobs

Saving means keeping money safe and reachable. It will not fall below what you put in, and you can withdraw it today. The trade-off is the rate: the average Nigerian savings account pays about 7.46%, well under inflation.

Investing means putting money somewhere it can grow faster than inflation. The trade-off is that you either lock it up for a period, or accept that the value moves up and down.

Neither is better. They answer different questions.

The question that decides it

When will I need this money? Within 12 months → savings account 1 to 3 years → savings bond or T-bill 3 years or more → equities, or a mix Time horizon picks the tool. Not the return.

What saving is genuinely for

Keep three to six months of living costs in an ordinary savings account. This is your emergency fund, and it is not an investment — its job is to be available on a bad day, not to grow.

Money you need within a year also belongs in savings. If you invest it and the market falls the month you need it, you are forced to sell at a loss.

Everything beyond those two buckets is a candidate for investing. The longer you can leave it, the more that matters.

Check yourself

Amaka has ₦200,000 set aside for her daughter’s school fees, due in 18 months. Where should it go?

  • NGX shares, to grow it as much as possible before the deadline
  • An FGN Savings Bond or Treasury Bill
  • A regular savings account, to keep it safe
Show the answer

Right. Eighteen months is long enough to earn a real return, but too short to absorb a market fall. A bond or T-bill gives a known return on a known date — which is exactly what a fixed deadline needs.

Do this now

Split your money into three lists: what you might need this month, what you need within a year, and what you will not touch for over a year.

Most people find the third list is far larger than they assumed — and that is the money currently earning 7.46% when it does not have to.
