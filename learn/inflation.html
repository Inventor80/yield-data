Why your money is shrinking right now

Prices in Nigeria rose 15.4% last year. The average savings account paid 7.5%. Here is what that gap actually costs you.
Ngozi finished last year feeling responsible. She saved ₦1,000,000 and did not touch it. By December her account showed ₦1,074,600 — she had earned 7.46% interest, the going savings rate. Then her landlord raised the shop rent from ₦1,000,000 to ₦1,153,900. Same shop. Same size. She did everything right and still came up ₦79,300 short.

The problem nobody told you about

Inflation is the rate at which prices across the economy rise. The National Bureau of Statistics measures it monthly by tracking what a standard basket of goods costs — food, rent, transport, clothing.

When inflation is 15.39%, something that cost ₦1,000 last year costs ₦1,154 today. Your money did not disappear. The number in your account stayed the same or grew slightly. But what that number can buy shrank.

What 15.39% actually does

₦1,000,000 in January. By December, you need ₦1,153,900 to buy the same things. If your savings account paid 7.46%, you have ₦1,074,600. You are ₦79,300 short of standing still.

Nominal vs real — the distinction that matters

Your nominal return is the number the bank quotes you: 7.46%.

Your real return is what is left after inflation eats its share: 7.46% minus 15.39% equals negative 7.93%.

This is why "my money is safe in the bank" is only half true. It is safe from theft. It is not safe from inflation. Nigerian inflation has come down a long way from the 30%+ of a few years ago — but a savings account still loses you about eight percent of your buying power every year.

Where each option leaves you

At 15.39% inflation, your real return is: Savings account (7.46%) → −7.93% 12-month deposit (10.30%) → −5.09% FGN Savings Bond, 2-year (13.071%) → −2.32% FGN Savings Bond, 3-year (14.071%) → −1.32% Treasury Bill (16.30%) → +0.91% Only the T-bill is genuinely ahead. But look at the spread: moving from the first line to the last is worth nearly nine percentage points a year, and none of it requires taking on market risk.

Check yourself

Inflation is 15.4%. Your savings account pays 7.5%. What is happening to your money in real terms?

  • It is growing — 7.5% is still a positive return
  • It is staying flat — you earned interest, so you broke even
  • It is shrinking — 7.5% is about 8 points below inflation
Show the answer

Exactly. The number in your account goes up while what it can buy goes down. That gap — almost 8 percentage points a year — is the silent cost of leaving money in a low-yield account.

Do this now

Open your banking app. Find your savings balance and the interest rate it pays. Then open the Yield inflation calculator, enter both numbers, and see the naira figure you are losing each year.

That number is the cost of doing nothing. It is usually larger than people expect.
