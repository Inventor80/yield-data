Your pension is an investment

For millions of Nigerians it is the largest pot of money they own, and most have never looked at it. If you are self-employed, you can open one too.
If you work for a registered employer, there is an account with your name on it, holding money that is yours, invested on your behalf, that you have probably never checked. Some people discover at retirement that it was the biggest investment of their life. Others discover their employer stopped paying into it years ago.

The money you already have

Under the Pension Reform Act, an employee has a Retirement Savings Account — an RSA — held at a Pension Fund Administrator you choose. Money goes in every month, from both you and your employer, and the PFA invests it in exactly the instruments you have been reading about: government bonds, Treasury Bills, shares, real estate.

So you already own a portfolio. You did not choose it, you may not know what is in it, and it is very probably growing.

Two things follow, and both are worth acting on.

It is yours, not your employer's. The account follows you between jobs. You do not open a new one each time you move; you give your new employer the PIN you already have.

You can check it. Your PFA will show you the balance and what it is invested in. An employer who is deducting your contribution and not remitting it is committing an offence, and you will only find out by looking.

Where the monthly money comes from

A share from your salary, before tax A larger share from your employer, on top of your salary Both land in your RSA and are invested The employer's share is money you never see on a payslip. It is still yours.

Why the tax treatment is the best in this app

Every instrument in Stage 2 so far has been taxed at one point or another. A pension is taxed favourably at both ends, and this is the reason to care.

Going in: from pre-tax income. Your contribution is deducted before your taxable income is worked out, so you are never taxed on it. Putting ₦50,000 into a pension does not cost you ₦50,000 of take-home pay — it costs less, because some of it would have gone in tax. The government is effectively paying part of your contribution.

While it grows: compounding, untouched, for decades. Stage 1's compounding lesson said the years matter more than the amount. A pension is the one account structured to run for thirty or forty of them without being dipped into.

Coming out: withdrawals on retirement, or from age 50, are tax-exempt under the 2026 rules.

Pre-tax in, decades of compounding, tax-free out. Nothing else available to an ordinary Nigerian saver does all three.

Why the years matter here more than anywhere

Fatima, from Stage 1, invested at 25 and had ₦14.9 million by 55. Kelvin put in twice as much at 35 and had ₦9.6 million. A pension is the account most likely to actually be left alone for thirty years — which is why it is where that arithmetic does its real work.

Self-employed? You can open one too

This is the part almost nobody knows, and it is the reason this lesson exists.

The Personal Pension Plan — for years called the Micro Pension Plan — lets anyone with an income open a pension, with no employer involved at all. Traders, drivers, hairdressers, freelancers, developers, anyone working for themselves.

There is no mandatory minimum contribution and no fixed schedule. You pay in what you can, when you can — after a good month, or on a regular day, whichever fits how you actually earn.

And the tax deduction still applies: contributions are deductible when you file your annual return, which for a self-employed person is one of the few reliefs genuinely available.

A practical starting rule, since there is no minimum to anchor to: 10% of every payment you receive, moved the same day it arrives. Not a budget you have to remember at month end — a reflex attached to money landing.

What it takes to start

1. Choose a Pension Fund Administrator 2. Open a Personal Pension Plan — your BVN, an ID, a phone number 3. Pay in whatever you choose, whenever you choose 4. Claim the deduction when you file your annual return No employer required, no minimum, no fixed schedule.

Getting at it, and the catch

The catch is real, and it is the same thing as the benefit: a pension is difficult to raid.

Retirement access is at 50, or earlier under specific circumstances, and the rules on how much can be drawn as a lump sum are set by PenCom rather than by you. Voluntary contributions have their own access rules, and those have been tightened in the past — so treat money you put in as locked away rather than as a savings account with a good rate.

That means a pension is not an emergency fund and not somewhere to put money for a house deposit in three years. Stage 1 said the horizon picks the tool. This is the longest horizon there is.

What you should do, whichever camp you are in:

Employed — find your PIN, log into your PFA, and look at the balance and whether contributions actually arrived each month. Also look at which fund you are in: PFAs run several, from more cautious to more growth-oriented, and younger savers are often in a more conservative one than they need to be. You can usually switch.

Self-employed — open a Personal Pension Plan. The hard part is the first day; after that it is a transfer.

Check yourself

Bisi runs her own tailoring business. She has no employer, so she assumes a pension is not available to her. What is actually true?

  • She is right — pensions require an employer to contribute
  • She can open a Personal Pension Plan herself, with no minimum, and deduct contributions from her taxable income
  • She can open one, but self-employed people get no tax relief
Show the answer

Correct — and this is the most under-used financial product in Nigeria. The Personal Pension Plan exists specifically for people earning outside formal employment. No minimum, no schedule, contributions deductible when she files her return, and withdrawals tax-exempt from 50.

Do this now

If you are employed: find your RSA PIN and log into your PFA today. Check two things — the balance, and whether a contribution has arrived every month this year. If months are missing, your employer has questions to answer, and you are the only person who will ask them.

If you are self-employed: search for Personal Pension Plan at any PFA and see what opening one requires. You will almost certainly find it is your BVN, an ID and a phone number.

Either way this takes fifteen minutes, and it is the highest-value quarter of an hour in this entire stage.
