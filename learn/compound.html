Compound interest — money that grows on itself

The difference between simple and compound growth looks trivial in year one and enormous by year twenty.
Invest ₦1,000,000 at 14% and never add another naira. How much is it worth in twenty years? Most people guess three or four million. The answer is closer to ₦13.7 million.

Earning on your earnings

Simple interest pays you on your original amount only. ₦1,000,000 at 14% pays ₦140,000 every year, forever.

Compound interest pays you on your original amount plus everything you have already earned. Year one pays ₦140,000. Year two pays 14% of ₦1,140,000 — that is ₦159,600. Year three pays on ₦1,299,600.

Each year the base grows, so each year earns more than the last. Early on the difference is small and boring. Later it becomes the whole story.

Same rate, same money, different time

₦1,000,000 at 14%: After 10 years Simple → ₦2,400,000 Compound → ₦3,707,000 After 20 years Simple → ₦3,800,000 Compound → ₦13,743,000 The only variable that changed is time.

Why starting early beats starting big

Because compounding accelerates, the years you give it matter more than the amount you begin with. This is the most useful thing to know if you are young and do not have much to invest yet.

Ten years of head start

Fatima invests ₦500,000 at 25. At 12% a year, by 55 she has about ₦14.9 million. Kelvin invests ₦1,000,000 at 35. Same rate. By 55 he has about ₦9.6 million. Fatima put in half as much and ends up with ₦5.3 million more. She simply started ten years earlier.

Check yourself

You invest ₦300,000 at 10% compound interest. How much interest have you earned after two years?

  • ₦60,000
  • ₦63,000
  • ₦66,000
Show the answer

Correct. Year one: 10% of ₦300,000 is ₦30,000. Year two: 10% of ₦330,000 is ₦33,000. Total ₦63,000 — the extra ₦3,000 is interest earned on interest. Small now; over twenty years that mechanism is what turns thousands into millions.
