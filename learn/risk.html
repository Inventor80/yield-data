What risk actually means

Risk is not the chance of losing everything. Understanding what it really is changes which choices feel sensible.
Ask most Nigerians why they avoid the stock market and you hear the same thing: "it is too risky, you can lose everything." That describes gambling. It does not describe investing — and the confusion stops people understanding their options at all.

Risk is uncertainty, not disaster

In finance, risk means uncertainty about the outcome — not the certainty of loss.

An FGN Savings Bond is low risk because you know the exact return on the exact date. A share in Dangote Cement is higher risk because nobody knows what it will be worth in three years. It could be much more. It could be less.

Higher risk is the price you pay for the possibility of higher returns. No instrument pays you well for taking no risk — if it did, everyone would hold it and the return would fall.

The risk ladder in Nigeria

Lowest risk → FGN Savings Bond. Government-backed, fixed rate, known date. Low → Treasury Bills. Same backing, shorter term. Medium → Mutual funds. Spread across many holdings. Higher → Individual shares. One company’s fortunes. Highest → A single share you bought on a tip.

The risk people forget

There is a second risk that never gets talked about: the risk of holding cash.

It feels like the safe option because the number never falls. But with inflation above 15%, cash earning 7.46% is the one thing guaranteed to lose value. Every other option at least has a chance of keeping up.

This is the trap. The choice that feels safest is the only one where the loss is certain.

Two kinds of risk

Shares: might fall 20% this year, might rise 30%. Uncertain. Cash at 7.46% with 15.39% inflation: loses about 8% of its buying power. Certain. One of these is a risk. The other is a slow, guaranteed loss.

Check yourself

Which of these carries the highest risk of losing purchasing power over ten years?

  • A diversified portfolio of NGX shares
  • A savings account paying 7.46% with inflation above 15%
  • A rolling ladder of Treasury Bills
Show the answer

Correct, and it surprises most people. Shares swing hard — the NGX All-Share Index fell in 3 of the 10 years to 2025 — yet over those 10 years it roughly kept pace with inflation. A savings account below inflation loses purchasing power every single year with no upside — the loss is not a risk, it is arithmetic.

Do this now

For each place your money currently sits, ask two questions: what is the worst realistic outcome, and what is the cost of leaving it exactly where it is?

The second question is the one almost nobody asks, and it is usually the one with the larger number.
